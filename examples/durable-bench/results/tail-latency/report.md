Tail-latency investigation, 2026-10-09 UTC. Candidate experiments are still running; no product speedup is claimed yet.

The initial distribution reproduces the warm-turn tail. It does not support blaming every outlier on maintenance overlap: all 960 Yielded warm turns entered with no previous alarm handler active. The largest outliers include slow admission, delayed model round trips, and slow intervals inside the mock provider itself. Maintenance still performs repeated work, so two small deletions are being measured separately.

Framework baseline: `07f0272e7ba49a494064b6b74c6318b55514ae19` (including #827). Harness: PR #828 at `78cb4eb7589984c1cd194e0cb01a5a0055a6d3f3`, with local instrumentation and resource naming changes. Its sole lagging framework file, `storage-cloudflare/src/internal/owned-state.ts`, was aligned to the framework baseline before the distribution bundle was built. Bench changes are confined to the separate bench checkout and this evidence branch. Product branches contain no bench code.

The distribution used 10 Objects per target/condition, one excluded warmup, and 24 warm turns per Object: 1,920 measured turns, 240 per cell. Each turn made nine model calls and eight sequential lookup tool calls. The 400 ms provider also delays chunks by 10 ms, totaling about 4,130 ms of programmed provider waiting per turn. Concurrency was 10 Objects. All timings come from deployed Cloudflare; local tests are correctness evidence only. All 1,920 warm samples passed transcript fingerprint and same-incarnation checks; no measured turn failed or was replayed. The seeded fingerprints remained `b017b487524e44a4` and `dcea9f30b0917245`.

The primary measurement is the deployed driver's submit-to-observed-settlement elapsed time. Quantiles use type-7 linear interpolation. A spike means a turn exceeds its own Object's warm median by more than 250 ms; the summaries also retain the 500 ms threshold. Maximum within-Object repeat ranges were 1,473 / 747 ms (Yielded / pi) at 50/instant, 2,079 / 895 ms at 250/instant, 965 / 1,107 ms at 50/400, and 1,926 / 535 ms at 250/400. These are wider than a four-repeat sample can reveal. Small changes inside that spread are not evidence of a speedup.

The deterministic workload was stable. Every Yielded warm turn used 34 storage transactions, including retirement; pi used 77 in nearly every turn. These are API operation counts, not a count of physical replication barriers. Yielded's SQL count was roughly 220–223 at 50 seeded turns and 257–261 at 250. The comparison does not imply that fewer SQL statements alone means lower latency.

Range rotation occurred once per Object, at warm repeat 12 for 50 seeded turns and repeat 2 for 250, matching the 1,024-record range bound. Rotation adds three scalar SQL statements and does not copy the archive. Three of the 40 rotation turns crossed the spike threshold; none of the largest outliers coincided with rotation. Archive export, lifecycle caching and application maintenance lanes are not configured in this workload. Their costs therefore cannot explain these particular measurements; this is not a performance claim about deployments that enable them.

FinalizationRegistry sentinels were registered at turn entry and model calls. Their callbacks appeared during essentially every turn in both frameworks. The counter measures collected sentinels, not GC cycles or pause duration, and does not prove that GC caused an outlier. The probe does not justify a speculative allocation rewrite. Cloudflare documents the [nondeterministic nature of finalization callbacks](https://blog.cloudflare.com/we-shipped-finalizationregistry-in-workers-why-you-should-never-use-it/).

Both builds in each A/B use identical instrumentation. The benchmark's target-side transcript verification clones, parses and hashes model requests inside the timer, so invocation CPU includes that work as well as framework execution. This investigation does not separately price that probe cost. That is a measurement-method lead for the bench owner, not an attributed product speedup.

Object `Date.now()` is an I/O clock and often does not advance across synchronous work or the maintenance tail. Provider and driver clocks are separate domains. Thus zero gaps are not zero-cost proof, and Object timestamps cannot be subtracted from driver timestamps to price settlement or storage precisely. Cloudflare's [wall-time definition](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/metrics-and-analytics/#wall-time-per-execution) also differs from client response latency. Overlapping alarm and await CPU/wall spans are reported separately, never summed into an exclusive total.

The provider anomaly is also visible across equal workloads: at 250/instant, repeat 11 sends the same 157,772-byte first request on all ten Objects, but Object 0 reports 801 ms of provider elapsed time while the others report 0–49 ms. The [neighboring-repeat slice](provider-slice.json) preserves that comparison. This rules out request size alone as an explanation for that isolated outlier; it does not distinguish upload delay, provider queuing, or another platform effect.

Across seeding and measurement, telemetry recorded 6,216 canceled alarms, 80 aborted fetches and 18 canceled fetches. The 80 explicit cold-reset calls account for the deliberate aborts. The remaining non-ok observations are retained rather than discarded; no measured application turn failed. The first collection had 143 unmatched markers, 208 ambiguous invocations, and small gaps in per-sample attribution, so CPU totals are not extrapolated from missing rows. Nearby canceled alarm events in the examined slow and typical cases had no handler marker. Current [workerd alarm scheduling](https://github.com/cloudflare/workerd/blob/baeb40cf80e31cd8588037bda77a20869387a4b9/src/workerd/io/worker.c++) distinguishes canceling a queued delivery from interrupting an active handler. That source helps interpret the logs but does not establish the exact deployed runtime version.

The enhanced A/B probe observes three physical `setAlarm` calls and a final `deleteAlarm` in a normal turn: ingress at now+50 ms, charged-wave fallback at now+1000 ms, and an advance to now+50 ms after actual progress. There are 31 transactions by `await:return` and 34 by `alarm:end`. Removing the fallback or progress advance would change recovery behavior. The native checkpoint and final gated reread protect producers that race with retirement. No relaxed durability, context cache, or new replay policy is part of either candidate.

Candidate 1, `514b83d00d4092d6df979612ca495846aae9606c`, suppresses only the duplicate in-memory mutation-gate notification for native-only progress owned by the active Attempt. Durable progress, alarms, ordinary wake hints, external producers and auxiliary lanes remain unchanged. The rigorous experiment uses the same 80 Objects, 12 warm repeats per pass, four BAAB passes, and 240 warm samples per build/condition. The result is pending.

Candidate 2, `413bb4bcb87352260b81ef647b6e3b89412b4220`, skips a maintenance-state write only when initialized native progress leaves the generation unchanged and no retry needs clearing. Initialization, external dirty increments, retry removal, source transactions and queue revisions remain intact. It is independent of candidate 1. Its measurement is pending.

Candidate 1 passed `vp run ready` in the product checkout with no vendored `third-party/node_modules`. The initial gate lacked a local Postgres service; an isolated temporary database supplied the required test dependency. Candidate 2's first full run had one 5-second recovery-test timeout with 140 other Cloudflare tests passing and six skips. That unchanged case passed in isolation at the existing deadline; the unchanged full gate then passed. No test deadline, assertion or suite was changed, and no new committed tests were added.

The initial distribution's target cleanup was verified by the Cloudflare API. Shared driver/provider resources are still serving the ongoing A/B experiments. Final teardown and `cleanup.json` remain pending.
